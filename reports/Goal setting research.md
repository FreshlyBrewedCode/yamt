# Evidence-based goal setting: energy and macro targets

Background research for yamt's guided goal setup (cut / maintain / bulk from body data and an activity self-assessment). Compiled 2026-11-03 from web research; items marked † were cited from secondary sources or memory and not re-checked against the primary source. URLs are listed at the end.

**In short:** use Mifflin-St Jeor for resting energy (Cunningham when body fat is known and the person is lean), estimate activity as a daily-life PAL plus a discounted exercise term rather than a single 1.2–1.9 multiplier, set the pace as % body weight per week (cut 0.5–1 %, lean bulk ~0.1–0.4 %), convert with 7,700 kcal/kg, set protein in g/kg, fat as a share of energy with a floor, carbohydrates as the remainder, fiber ≥ 30 g. Any equation is ±10 % for an individual (≈ 250 kcal), so the result is a starting point that a weight-trend based (adaptive) estimate should take over after 3–4 weeks.

## 1. Resting energy (RMR/BMR)

W = weight kg, H = height cm (m where noted), A = age y, FFM = fat-free mass kg, sex = 1 male / 0 female; kcal/day.

| Equation | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mifflin-St Jeor 1990† | 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5 (m) / −161 (f) | Frankenfield 2005 review: most accurate in non-obese and obese adults (~78 % within ±10 %, vs 67 % Harris-Benedict). Default. |
| Harris-Benedict revised (Roza & Shizgal 1984)† | m 88.362 + 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A; f 447.593 + 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A | Less accurate than Mifflin. |
| Cunningham 1980† | 500 + 22·FFM | Most accurate in recreational athletes (ten Haaf 2014); used by MacroFactor. |
| Katch-McArdle (= Cunningham 1991)† | 370 + 21.6·FFM | |
| ten Haaf & Weijs 2014 (athletes 18–35) | 11.936W + 587.73·H(m) − 8.130A + 191.03·sex + 29.28; FFM form 22.771·FFM + 484.3 | Most precise in O'Neill 2023 meta-analysis (80 % within ±10 %). |
| Tinsley 2019 (muscular athletes) | 24.8W + 10; or 25.9·FFM + 284 | |
| Müller 2004 (German data)† | (0.047W + 1.009·sex − 0.01452A + 3.21) MJ × 239 | Attributed to DGE by German secondary sources. |

FFM-only equations ignore fat mass and under-predict at high body fat: prefer Mifflin there. Consumer BIA body fat is good enough (5 points error ≈ 90 kcal on Cunningham).

**Worked example** (test fixture): man, 30 y, 180 cm, 80 kg, 15 % body fat (FFM 68 kg): Mifflin 1,780; Katch-McArdle 1,839; Cunningham 1,996; Tinsley 1,994; ten Haaf (weight) 1,989.

## 2. Activity

- The 1.2 / 1.375 / 1.55 / 1.725 / 1.9 multipliers trace to a textbook (McArdle/Katch/Katch 1996†) and have no validation study; "sedentary 1.2" is below doubly-labelled-water reference values (sedentary/light PAL 1.40–1.69, FAO/WHO/UNU 2004†).
- FAO/WHO/UNU: sedentary/light 1.40–1.69, active 1.70–1.99, vigorous 2.00–2.40; above ~2.4 is hard to sustain.
- DGE examples†: 1.4–1.5 office work with little leisure activity; 1.6–1.7 sitting with some walking/standing; 1.8–1.9 mostly standing/walking; 2.0–2.4 heavy work or competitive sport; +0.3 for 4–5 × 30–60 min sport per week.
- NASEM 2023 replaced the IOM equations with doubly-labelled-water based TEE equations per activity category.
- **Factorial structure (better than one multiplier):** TEE = RMR × daily-life PAL (exercise excluded) + net exercise energy, net exercise per session = (MET − 1) × hours × RMR/24 (MET values: 2024 Adult Compendium, Herrmann 2024).
- **Discount exercise (~0.75):** Careau 2021 (n = 1,754) found ~28 % of activity energy compensated by lower basal expenditure; self-reports overstate activity (Lichtman 1992; Prince 2008).
- Established tools: NIH Body Weight Planner asks work and leisure activity (PAL 1.4–2.5); MacroFactor asks daily-life steps, sessions per week, cardio and lifting experience, optional body fat; DGE uses occupation PAL plus a sport adder.
- Steps (Tudor-Locke & Bassett 2004): < 5,000 sedentary, 5,000–7,499 low active, 7,500–9,999 somewhat active, ≥ 10,000 active. Wearable step counts are reliable; wearable energy estimates are not (Evenson & Spade 2020 for Garmin; O'Driscoll 2020; Shcherbina 2017†): use steps, treat device calories as a noisy relative signal only.

## 3. Pace per goal

- **Cut:** 0.5–1.0 % body weight per week (Helms, Aragon & Fitschen 2014); Garthe 2011: 0.7 %/wk kept and gained lean mass, 1.4 %/wk did not. General population: ~500 kcal/day deficit (German S3 obesity guideline 2014), 500–750 (AHA/ACC/TOS 2013).
- **Bulk:** ~0.25–0.5 %/wk for novice/intermediate, less for advanced (Iraki 2019); 5 % vs 15 % surplus gave similar gains with more fat at 15 % (Helms 2023); no evidence for > 500 kcal/day (Slater 2019). MacroFactor rates (%/wk, conservative / medium / aggressive): beginner 0.2 / 0.5 / 0.8, intermediate 0.15 / 0.325 / 0.575, experienced 0.1 / 0.15 / 0.35.
- **Conversion:** 7,700 kcal/kg (≈ 78/22 fat/lean mix; Hall 2008 energy densities fat 9,440, lean 1,820 kcal/kg). Static rule overestimates long-term change (Hall 2011, Thomas 2013), but fine when weekly weight data closes the loop.
- **Floors:** ~1,200 kcal (women) / 1,500 kcal (men) minimum targets (AHA ranges); < 800 kcal only under medical supervision; IOC REDs 2023: energy availability ≤ 30 kcal/kg FFM/day is a high-risk zone; cap cuts at 1 %/wk.

## 4. Macros

- **Protein:** Morton 2018 (49 RCTs): gains plateau ~1.62 g/kg (CI to ~2.2); ISSN (Jäger 2017) 1.4–2.0 g/kg for exercising people; in a deficit and lean, 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM (Helms 2014); bulk 1.6–2.2 g/kg (Iraki 2019). Floors: DGE 0.8 g/kg (65+: 1.0), EFSA 0.83; older adults 1.0–1.2 (PROT-AGE†). DGE: above BMI 25 calculate on normal (reference) weight. Useful upper limit ~2.2–2.5 g/kg.
- **Fat:** EFSA/IOM 20–35 % of energy; DGE 30 %; ACSM 2016 not below 20 %; contest prep 15–30 % (Helms 2014); lowering fat from ~40 % to ~20 % lowered men's testosterone ~10–15 % (Whittaker & Wu 2021). Default 30 %, floor max(20 %, ~0.6 g/kg).
- **Carbohydrates:** the remainder. References: DGE > 50 %, EFSA 45–60 %, IOM 45–65 % (RDA 130 g†). Athletes (ACSM/AND/DC 2016†): light 3–5, moderate ~1 h/day 5–7, endurance 1–3 h/day 6–10 g/kg: use as warnings, not as the driver.
- **Fixed % splits (40/30/30 …) are not evidence-based:** at equal energy the macro mix barely changes weight loss (Sacks 2009†; S3 guideline), and percentages scale badly (30 % protein at 3,500 kcal = 262 g; 30 % fat at 1,400 kcal ≈ 0.6 g/kg). Base protein and the fat floor on g/kg and carbs as remainder; show the resulting % split.
- **Fiber:** DGE 2021 ≥ 30 g/day (≈ 14.6 g/1000 kcal); IOM 14 g/1000 kcal†; EFSA 25 g.
- **Labelling:** EU "Kohlenhydrate" exclude fiber (2 kcal/g); USDA "Carbohydrate, by difference" includes it. Energy check: 4P + 4C + 9F + 2Fib.

## 5. German / EU reference values

DGE (energy, derived 2015, reference BMI 22): ages 25–51 women 1,800 / 2,100 / 2,400 kcal, men 2,300 / 2,700 / 3,000 kcal at PAL 1.4 / 1.6 / 1.8. Protein 0.8 g/kg (65+: 1.0). Fat 30 %. Carbohydrates > 50 %. Fiber ≥ 30 g. S3 obesity guideline: −500 kcal/day and > 150 min activity/week. EFSA: protein 0.83 g/kg, fat 20–35 %, carbs 45–60 %, fiber 25 g. DGE published a 3rd edition of its reference values in 2025: re-check before relying on exact numbers.

## 6. Adaptive expenditure (for a later retro feature)

TDEE ≈ mean logged intake − (trend weight change × 7,700) / days. MacroFactor: updates from day 3, converges in 14–30 days; after calibration median error 135 kcal vs 335 for formulas; pauses when > 3 of 7 days are unlogged; consistent logging bias is absorbed. Hacker's Diet: EWMA alpha 0.1. With ~0.6 kg day-to-day noise, an OLS slope over 7 / 14 / 28 days gives roughly ±870 / ±310 / ±110 kcal/day uncertainty, so ~3–4 weeks are needed. Hand-over: a Kalman-style blend of the formula prior (SD ~12 %) and the windowed estimate, limited to ±150 kcal/week changes, skipping ~7 days after big intake changes. Open source to read: SparkyFitness (28-day adaptive TDEE), tdee-adaptive (Go), the `bw` R package (Hall model).

## 7. Edge cases and safety

Refuse: under 18; pregnant or breastfeeding; a cut at BMI < 18.5. Flag: kidney disease (protein), insulin/sulfonylureas (cuts), GLP-1 drugs, BMI ≥ 40. Older adults: protein ≥ 1.0–1.2 g/kg, gentle cuts. Calorie tracking correlates with eating-disorder symptoms (Simpson & Mazzeo 2017; Levinson 2017; Linardon & Messer 2019): rate caps, floors, neutral wording (no praise for undershooting).
